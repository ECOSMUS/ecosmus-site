Privacy Policy

Last updated 5 November 2026. What data Ecosmus uses, why, and where it lives.

The short version

Ecosmus keeps what you create on your phone. We do not have user accounts, we do not run advertising or analytics trackers, and we do not sell your data.

This policy explains what Ecosmus handles, why, and the choices and rights you have.

Who is responsible

Paulo Rodrigo Cossuol is responsible for Ecosmus. Contact: hello@ecosmus.com. For privacy questions: privacy@ecosmus.com.

What Ecosmus handles

• Things you create: ideas, reminders, actions, shopping items, your other lists, notes, and which ones you have completed. Stored on your phone.

• Profile details you choose to enter: full name, nickname, date of birth, sex, address. All optional. Stored on your phone and used on the phone to personalise greetings and, in future, suggestions.

• Calendar events: Ecosmus reads and writes events in the calendars you allow, through your phone calendar. We do not copy them anywhere else.

• Location: only if you allow it. Ecosmus uses it to show the weather where you are, to tell you when you arrive at or leave one of your saved shops, and (if you turn on "Notice shops I visit") to spot when you have stopped at a shop for a few minutes. If you choose "Always", iOS watches circles around your saved shops and wakes Ecosmus when you arrive or leave. Ecosmus keeps no record of where you go: it keeps only the shops you save, with the dates and times of your visits there.

• My Shops: the shops you save, their address, your notes and preferences, your visits, and what you tick off your shopping list while you are there. Stored on your phone.

• Money: the bills, accounts, income, budget buckets and spending you enter or tell Cosmo. Ecosmus only connects to your bank if you choose to link it (below), and never asks for your bank login, account or card numbers: an account is just a name, and the last four digits if you choose. Stored on your phone (and in your iCloud copy, if it is on). If you turn on Show it in my calendar, a repeating event is added to the calendar you chose, titled as privately as you set (name and amount, name only, or "Bill due"). Alerts on your lock screen follow the same choice. Cosmo uses your bills to answer questions such as "What bills are this week?"; with Cosmo Cloud on, the bill names, amounts and dates needed to answer are sent as described below.

• Money coach (Money › Plan): it works out what's safe to spend, plans for goals and general ideas from your own numbers, on your iPhone. Questions it can't answer itself go to Apple Intelligence on your iPhone when available (Apple's own terms apply); never to Cosmo Cloud. It gives general guidance, not regulated financial advice, and doesn't recommend products or providers. Money can be hidden on screen and locked with Face ID, Touch ID or your PIN.

• Linked banks and cards (Money, Premium, off unless you link one): through Apple's FinanceKit, Apple connects to your bank (in the UK through open banking) and you choose which accounts to share. Ecosmus reads account names, balances and transactions (date, amount, description, merchant) on your iPhone; it never sees your bank login and can never move money or make payments. This data is not sent to Ecosmus or anyone else; it is kept with the rest of your Money data on your phone (and in your iCloud copy, if it is on). Stop sharing at any time in iPhone Settings › Privacy & Security › Financial Data, or unlink in Money.

• Store cards: the loyalty card numbers you add (never payment cards). Stored on your phone and shown as a barcode when you need it. If you scan a card or choose a screenshot of one, the picture is read on your phone and not kept.

• Contact details (optional): your mobile, WhatsApp and email in About me, typed by you or copied from the contact card you pick. Kept on your phone.

• Invitations (optional): if you choose Invite Contacts, the iOS contact picker returns only the person you select. Their name and phone number are used on your iPhone to prepare a text in Messages; Ecosmus does not send it for you. Share Link opens the iOS share sheet. Ecosmus does not upload your contacts, receive recipient or delivery information, or count invitations.

• Profile picture (optional): a photo you take or choose, kept only in Ecosmus on your phone. Remove it any time in About Me > Your details.

• Check-ins and journal: your replies when Cosmo asks how an event went or whether you got everything at a shop, and what Cosmo did with them. Stored on your phone.

• Weather places: the towns and cities you add to the weather page. Stored on your phone.

• Home and work: if you set them for "when I get home" reminders, their position stays on your phone.

• Apple Intelligence: on iPhones that have it, Cosmo uses Apple's on-device model to understand what you say and to answer questions. This runs on your iPhone; Ecosmus does not send it anywhere.

• Voice: when you tap the microphone, your phone converts speech to text using its built-in speech recognition. Depending on your phone and language settings, Apple may process the audio on its servers under its own privacy policy. Ecosmus receives and keeps only the text, not the audio.

• Progress: counts of captured and completed items are worked out on your phone.

• Where you noted things (on unless you turn it off in About Me › Cosmo): each note keeps the position your iPhone already knew (rounded to about 10 m) and a place name such as "Bank station". Stored on your phone.

• What you buy and how often: the dates you tick shopping items off, so Cosmo can suggest "Running low?". Stored on your phone.

• Journeys, the smart alarm and closures: your usual journeys, alarm times and the closures that affect you. Stored on your phone; the alarm is set with iOS on your phone.

• Siri and widgets (full app): Ecosmus keeps a copy of your shopping list and your line names in a private area on your iPhone that its Siri actions and widgets can read. Nothing leaves your phone.

• iCloud backup (full app, on unless you turn it off): a copy of your lists, shops, journeys, journal and settings is saved in your own private iCloud database. Apple keeps it under your Apple Account and we cannot read it. Your PIN and any keys are never copied. Turn it off in About Me › Your data and iCloud.

• Notifications: reminders, check-ins and the morning briefing are created and scheduled on your phone. Nothing is sent from our servers.

• Subscription: if you take Ecosmus Premium, Apple handles the payment. We receive no card details, only confirmation from Apple that your subscription is active.

• Licence: if you link your Apple Account (Sign in with Apple), Ecosmus keeps Apple's private identifier for it, the anonymous device IDs of up to 2 iPhones and their model names, only to check your Premium licence.

Why we use it and our legal basis

To provide the app you asked for (performance of a contract), and, for optional items such as your profile, location, microphone, calendar and notifications, with your consent. You give consent by entering the details or allowing the permission, and you can withdraw it at any time by deleting the details or switching the permission off in your phone Settings. Withdrawing does not affect what happened before.

Who it is shared with

We do not sell or share your data for advertising. To work, Ecosmus contacts these services directly from your phone, without any Ecosmus account or identifier:

• Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com), for weather forecasts, air quality, pollen and place search. Ecosmus sends a position rounded to about 1 km, or the town name you search for.

• Transport for London (api.tfl.gov.uk), for My Travels: Ecosmus asks for the status of your lines, planned closures and the next trains at your stations, searches stations by name, and, to recognise the station you are at, sends a position rounded to about 10 metres to TfL. For walking times, leave-by alerts and plan B it uses TfL's Journey Planner, sending your stations and the positions of home, work or the event, rounded to about 100 metres. Nothing about you is sent.

• Nager.Date (date.nager.at), for public holidays: only a country code and a year are sent.

• Open Food Facts (openfoodfacts.org), only when you scan a packet or a photo you give Cosmo shows a product barcode: Ecosmus sends the barcode number to look up the product name. Nothing else about you or the photo is sent.

• Recipe pages, only when you add a recipe to Shopping: Ecosmus opens the page you chose to read its ingredients. The site sees a normal visit to that page.

• Open Prices by Open Food Facts (prices.openfoodfacts.org), only for Price watch (Premium) on food and household items: Ecosmus sends the item name and your price currency to read real prices that shoppers have logged at shops. Nothing about you or your location is sent.

• The shops you track in Price watch (Premium): Ecosmus opens the product pages you chose, in its own browser or in the background, to read the price. The shop sees a normal visit to that page. Searches you make there go to that shop or to Google Shopping.

• Apple Maps (through the iPhone), when you type an address or move a pin, to find the place and its address, and to find where a calendar event with an address takes place (for leave-by alerts).

• Apple (Siri, and Apple's cloud model when it is available in the full app): what you say to Siri is handled by Apple under its own privacy policy, and Ecosmus receives only the result. If Apple's cloud model is on, what you say to Cosmo is processed by Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which Apple says does not keep your data.

• RevenueCat (only in the App Store version, when you buy or restore Premium): Apple sends your purchase receipt to RevenueCat, which checks it and tells Ecosmus whether Premium is active on your iPhones. RevenueCat sees a random app user number and your purchase history with Ecosmus, never your name, lists, bills or anything else you put in the app. RevenueCat's own privacy policy applies.

• Anthropic (Claude, Premium, only where the app offers it and off unless you switch it on): what you say to Cosmo, and the facts needed to answer (such as your events, list titles, shops and weather), are sent to Anthropic's Claude to understand and answer that request. Anthropic's own terms and privacy policy apply. Switch it off at any time in About Me › Cosmo.

• Ecosmus support, only if you choose Report an issue: an email you see before it is sent, with app version, iPhone model, which features and permissions are on, counts of your items and recent errors (with emails, phone numbers and postcodes blanked out). No lists, names or messages.

• Invitations, only if you choose to share: the iOS contact picker provides the one contact you select so Ecosmus can prepare a text in Messages. You decide whether to send it. Share Link opens the iOS share sheet, and the destination you choose receives the link. Ecosmus has no referral backend: it cannot tell who received or opened an invitation and does not track or count invites.

• OpenStreetMap (via the Overpass API), to find the name of a shop you are standing in (a position rounded to about 10 m, only when you add a shop or when "Notice shops I visit" spots a stop), for "Shops on my way" (Premium, off unless you switch it on: shops of the right kind and their opening hours within a short walk, a position rounded to about 10 m), and, in Price watch, real shops of the right kind near your home (a position rounded to about 100 m).

Like any website, these services see your internet address when your phone connects. Your phone and its providers also handle some things as part of the system: Apple (notifications, speech recognition, payments, device backups if you use iCloud Backup) and the providers of any calendar you connect, such as iCloud, Google or Microsoft. Their own privacy policies apply to that.

How long we keep it

On your phone until you delete it. You can download it (a readable report or a data file) or delete everything at any time in About Me › Your data and iCloud, or by removing the app. The app log keeps only its last 300 entries.

Security

Data stays in the app storage on your phone, protected by your phone passcode and iOS security. Your Ecosmus PIN is never stored as typed: only a salted, scrambled check of it, in the iPhone Keychain, and after several wrong tries Ecosmus makes you wait. A Cosmo Cloud key, if you add one, is also kept in the Keychain. "Hide content" on the Today page covers your information when others can see your screen. Please keep your phone locked and updated.

Automated decisions

Ecosmus sorts what you say into ideas, reminders, actions and lists, understands your replies to check-ins, and gives weather tips, using rules and, where available, Apple Intelligence on your phone, Cosmo Cloud by Apple (Apple's Private Cloud Compute) or, if offered and you switch it on, Anthropic's Claude. This does not have legal or similarly significant effects, and you can change or delete the result at any time.

Your rights

Under UK and EU data protection law you can ask to access, correct, delete, restrict, move or object to the use of your personal data, and withdraw consent. Because your data is on your phone, you can do most of this yourself in Settings (see "Your data rights"). You can also write to us at the address above.

You can complain to the UK Information Commissioner (ico.org.uk, 0303 123 1113) or, in Portugal, to the Comissão Nacional de Proteção de Dados (cnpd.pt), or to the regulator where you live.

Children

Ecosmus is not intended for anyone under 16. If you are under 16, please ask a parent or guardian to use it with you.

Changes

If we change how Ecosmus uses data (for example by adding an account, cloud backup or AI features), we will update this policy and ask for your agreement where the law requires it. Last updated 5 November 2026.
